    Designed in simulation

    The airframe was modeled in OpenRocket: a 162.6 mm fiberglass ogive nose, a 624 mm forward bay and a 900 mm fin can. Flights were simulated with a 1 m launch rod and 2 m/s wind. These renders show the model in OpenRocket's default colors, before the final paint.

    Modeling the fin can

    The fin can is the 900 mm aft section. It carries the motor mount, the parachute and the fin set, so it was modeled first.

    Printing the fins

    Three trapezoidal fins, printed in PETG at 100% infill and 4 mm thick. Root chord 177.8 mm, tip chord 50.8 mm, span 50.8 mm, sweep 25.4 mm.

    The bulkhead

    A bulkhead in the lower body holds the fin can and body together, and still lets ejection gas through.

    RetainsIt locks the fin can to the body so the two sections stay together.
    Inspired by VirgoThe design follows the bulkhead from MIT's Virgo rocket.
    PortedUnlike the Virgo bulkhead, it is open to ejection gases, so they pass through to the recovery system.

    Inside the airframe

    Forward bay624 mm filament-wound fiberglass. Avionics sled, altimeter, switch and battery.
    Motor mount380 mm fiberglass tube with an AeroPack RA38P retainer.
    Recovery36 in ripstop nylon parachute, Nomex protector and shock cord.
    Nose coneFiberglass ogive, 162.6 mm long.
    Rail buttonsTwo Delrin buttons.
